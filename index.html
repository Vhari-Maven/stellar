Physics in this model. The star is 20 concentric mass shells (Lagrangian coordinates), each tracking its own H/He/Z composition. A fixed temperature profile T(m̃) = T_c · (1 − m̃)^β with β = 2 makes burning concentrate near the centre; the central temperature scales as T_c ∝ μ_central · M^0.3 from hydrostatic equilibrium, where μ = 1/(2X + ¾Y + ½Z) and μcentral is taken at the innermost shell. Each shell burns at L_i ∝ X_i² · T_i⁴ · m_i (pp-chain temperature dependence), with the calibration constant fixed so a pristine 1 M☉ star starts at 1 L☉. As shells fill with helium, μ at the centre rises, T_c rises, and L grows; once an inner shell exhausts hydrogen the burning region migrates outward. The "core" / "envelope" labels in the readout are just diagnostics — the inner 20% / outer 80% of mass — and have no dynamical role. Surface Teff is back-calculated from L and a rough mass-radius relation R ∝ M0.7. Things this toy still ignores: convection zones, opacity, gravitational settling, the CNO cycle, real shell burning energetics, and any structural response to mass loss — so interventions (mining, helium extraction) move the star around in a way that's qualitatively right but isn't a self-consistent stellar evolution code.
