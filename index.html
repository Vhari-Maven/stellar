Physics in this model. The core occupies the inner 20% of the star by mass. Hydrogen burns there at rate dM_H/dt = −L / (0.007 c²), the actual mass-energy yield of the pp-chain. Luminosity follows a homology relation L ∝ M^3.5 · (μ/μ₀)⁴, where μ = 1/(2X + ¾Y + ½Z) is the mean molecular weight of fully ionized gas. As helium accumulates, μ rises; the core has fewer particles per unit mass, so to maintain hydrostatic pressure the temperature climbs and L grows. The star ends the main sequence when Xcore falls below 0.05. Surface Teff is back-calculated from L and a rough mass-radius relation R ∝ M^0.7. Things this toy ignores: convection zones, Kramers/electron-scattering opacity, gravitational settling, the CNO cycle (matters above ~1.3 M☉), shell burning, and the gradual outward march of the burning region — which is why a realistic 10 Gyr lifetime requires a slightly inflated 20% core fraction here. The qualitative response to your interventions is what comes through cleanly.
